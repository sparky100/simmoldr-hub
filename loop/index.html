The problem

Feedback doesn't fit in a spreadsheet.

Customers, cash, staff, stock, infections: each one builds up over time and changes the rate at which it changes. A row of formulas can describe one step. It can't easily show you what the loop does over years.

  • 🛢️
    Things accumulate

    A stock carries its history with it. Today's backlog is every order that came in minus every order that went out, so the past keeps shaping the present.

  • 🔁
    Loops push and pull

    Reinforcing loops drive growth and collapse. Balancing loops hold things steady. Which one is in charge can change part way through, and that is usually where the surprise is.

  • ⏳
    Delays mislead

    When the effect of a decision arrives months later, people over-correct. Delays are built into Loop, so you can see the overshoot before it happens.

And if your question doesn't really involve feedback, Loop will tell you. Its Loop or Excel? check reads the model's equations and says whether a spreadsheet would do the job, and why.

Worked example

An epidemic, from structure to behaviour.

The classic SIR model is one of Loop's built-in templates. Three stocks of people (susceptible, infected and recovered) and two flows between them.

Run it and the feedback shows itself: infection grows on itself until the susceptible pool runs low, then the balancing loop takes over and the outbreak burns out.

01 — Design
The structure, in classic notation

Stocks are boxes, flows are pipes with valves, and clouds mark where material enters or leaves the system. Switch the view to add the auxiliaries, constants and causal links behind each flow.

02 — Run
The behaviour that structure produces

The infected curve peaks as the susceptible pool runs down. Change the transmission rate on a slider and the peak moves straight away; save each setting as a scenario to compare later.

Built for real models

Simple to start. Readable when it grows.

Sub-models with a stated boundary

Group a model into sub-models and declare what each one takes in and feeds out. Loop describes how each part works, warns when something crosses the boundary without a port, and can test a sub-model on its own.

Follow the dependencies

In a large model, pick a sub-model and see everything it reads and everything that reads it, arranged by where it lives. Download it as an image or print it.

Share

FAQ

Questions about Loop

Do I need to know system dynamics already?

No. Describe what you're modelling in plain language and Loop drafts a first version for you to review, or start from one of the templates. The user guide explains stocks, flows and loops as you meet them.

Can I bring models from Vensim or Stella?

Yes. Loop imports XMILE files, including arrays of any number of dimensions, and Vensim .mdl files without subscripts. Before the model is created it shows a report of what it changed, what it left out and what needs your review. Models also export as XMILE and JSON.

Do I have to use the AI features?

No. You can build everything by hand on the canvas or in the forms, and the model checks work with no AI configured. The AI features are there to get you started faster and to explain results.

Can I use my own AI assistant?

Yes. Loop's MCP server lets an assistant such as Claude build, validate, run, test and document Loop models. It runs locally over plain JSON model files, with no account needed and no data leaving your machine. There is also a downloadable prompt pack for chat tools.

Where do my models live?

Models are saved to your Loop account and stay private unless you share them. You can invite named viewers or editors, make a model public, or publish it as a simulator. Every run keeps its own snapshot of the model, so history survives later edits.

What can't it do yet?

Loop doesn't yet read data from Excel, databases or live sources (data comes in as CSV), there is no live co-editing, and integration is Euler or RK4 only. We'd rather say so than have you find out later.
