The problem

Workshops end with notes, not understanding.

Everyone leaves the room with a different picture. The sticky notes get photographed, the transcript sits in a folder, and the loudest view becomes the agreed one by default.

  • 🗒️
    Notes pile up

    Interviews, meeting notes and sticky walls each hold part of the story. Nobody has time to join them up, so the same point gets made five times and the important one gets lost.

  • ⚖️
    Disagreement gets smoothed over

    A summary picks one version. Lens keeps each person's view and the evidence for and against it on the map, so a real difference of opinion is something you can see and talk about.

  • ❓
    Assumptions pass as facts

    "Everyone knows that" is often a guess. Lens separates what you have evidence for from what you are taking for granted, and points out the claims nothing backs up.

How it works

From a pile of notes to a map you can act on.

01

Bring in what you have.

Paste notes or a transcript, record the room, type a fast outline, or let Lens interview you one question at a time.

02

Review the first draft.

AI suggests concerns, goals, actions, people, assumptions and evidence, and how they link. Nothing goes on the map until you accept it.

03

Decide what to do.

Vote, prioritise and track actions on a board, then send a plain summary to the people who weren't in the room.

Capabilities

Built for the whole conversation, not just the wall of stickies.

One map holds six kinds of thing, joined by typed links: concerns, goals, actions, people, assumptions and evidence. Everything else in Lens is a different way into that map.

🗺️

One shared map

Draw it by hand, or start from a template. Filter it, search it and merge duplicates, so the picture stays clear as it grows.

💬

Interviews that dig

Five interview styles: open questions, one person's point of view, why-and-how laddering, describing the system, and what's keeping a decision stuck.

🎭

Every viewpoint kept

Record who said what and whose view a node comes from. Toggle viewpoints on the map to see what each person or role contributed, and who has been left out.

🔍

A second pair of eyes

Facilitator Review flags gaps as you go: a concern with no evidence, an action with no owner, a decision that has stalled.

🎯

From talk to action

Vote, rank goals and actions by priority, compare alternatives, and follow delivery on a board. Every status change records who decided and when.

🧑‍🏫

Help running the room

A facilitator guide paces a live session stage by stage, and a simple coaching mode offers guided conversations for one-to-ones and team sessions.

👥

Work with others

Share a map as owner, editor, viewer or facilitator. See who else is on the map, and never overwrite someone's edit by accident.

🤖

Your AI, or none

Use Lens's built-in AI, take the prompt pack to the assistant you already use and bring the result back, or build everything by hand.

📤

Take it with you

Export the map as Markdown, HTML, JSON-LD or Graph JSON, or send a written status report to people who weren't there.

Who it's for

For anyone who has to make sense of a messy situation with other people.

FAQ

Questions about Lens

How do I start?

Open lens.simmodlr.app, sign in and create a map, or take the guided tour on a ready-made demo map. Lens is in its early days, so tell us what works and what doesn't at support@simmodlr.app.

Do I need to know systems thinking?

No. Lens is written for facilitators, consultants and team members with no background in formal methods. The interview styles draw on established problem-structuring approaches, but the app uses plain names for them.

Do I have to use AI?

No. Everything can be built by hand. If you do use AI, it only suggests: every suggested item waits for you to accept it, and stays marked until someone confirms it.

Can I use the AI assistant I already have?

Yes. Download the prompt pack, paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or similar, have the conversation there, and bring the result back into Lens as a ready-made map.

How many people can work on a map at once?

Lens is designed for a small group working together, such as a workshop or a team, with live presence and editing locks so two people don't change the same thing at once.

Can I get my map out again?

Yes. Export to Markdown, HTML, JSON-LD or Graph JSON at any time. Graph JSON can be imported back into Lens, so nothing is locked in.
